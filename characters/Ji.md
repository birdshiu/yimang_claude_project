**Ji** (traditional Chinese: 姬; pinyin: *Jī*) is one of the Sols of the Tiandao Council. He holds the Sol Seal of Insight.

## Appearance

Ji is a solarian with snow white fur and a thin, androgynous build. He wears a yellow garment that exposes some of his sides, as well as his arms, hips, and legs, with a loincloth hanging in front and behind. He also wears a large red and and yellow fez-like hat that descends into a cloak behind his back with golden honeycomb patterns at the bottom. On the front of the hat hang two smaller red tassels, each tipped with gold. The band of his hat appears to be made of a thick jade ring, featuring a large, moving eye on the front. Ji's fingers are tipped with long, jade claws. He possesses a levitating orb that resembles an Armillary sphere, which he uses during divination.

## Story

### Past

Also known as the Kunlun Immortal, Ji is an enigmatic figure with a long history. His exact origins are unknown, but he is known to have been alive for many thousands of years, having seen the rise and fall of countless great historical figures. At some point early in his life, perhaps as a child, he was worshipped for his potent abilities of divination, likely among his other supernatural abilities, such as his agelessness.

Throughout his life, he was part of many different groups, and witnessed many historically significant events. In the Turbulent Era, Ji assisted Jietong, the final emperor of the Jie Kingdom, as the seer of the Jie Kingdom. The day the Fangshi Guild overthrew the oppressive reign of the emperor, Ji was present when Jietong wrote the Parting Missive and during his subsequent suicide. After the Turbulent Era came to an end and Origin Era was proclaimed, Ji became affiliated with Lear and his Fangshi Guild. He served as the deputy captain of the Fangshi Guild Elite Guard, and worked with Lear on science and technology research. In the 10th year of the Origin Era, the day when Lear disbanded the Fangshi Guild, Ji was present during Lear's Inaction Declaration. He was also later beside Lear during his departure from the living realm to the Limitless Realm.

Ji spent a long time secluded in the Grotto of Scriptures to protect Lear's achievements. To preserve the memories he wished to remember, Ji recorded his recollections of events upon black stone steles within the Ancient Stone Pillar. Originally beginning as a casual hobby, this endeavor gradually became a behemoth of information: the sheer quantity of these steles is said to span the entirety of the Solarian civilization, including its rise and fall.

Because of his long lifespan, Ji experienced the repeated loss of all of the people he ever cared about, as they all eventually died. Consequently, he became deeply envious of those who ended their lives spectacularly, and developed a deep yearning for a magnificent curtain call of his own.

### Life as a Sol

Ji became a member of the Tiandao Council, and subsequently a Sol, in order to protect the Grotto of Scriptures.

Ji once met Kuafu, and foretold the engineer's fortune; he determined that Kuafu would spend the rest of his life away from his hometown. Kuafu rejected the validity of this fortune, considering it to be nonsense.

Ji was present for the two Council meetings where Yi proposed the Eternal Cauldron Project. He remained silent and did not participate in the discussions of either meeting. As part of the project, he was given administration over the Grotto of Scriptures.

At some point, Ji was apparently informed by Eigong that she was responsible for the synthesis and subsequent pandemic of Tianhuo Virus, and chose to continue cooperating with her. While unaffected by the effects of the Tianhuo, Ji allowed Eigong to experiment on him in her search for a cure for Tianhuo. On the eve of New Kunlun's launch, Eigong made a breakthrough in her research with data from Ji's cells; she believed it could be not only be used as a cure for Tianhuo, but also be used to achieve true immortality. From this research, Eigong developed the Tianhuo Serum, which she believed could potentially cure Tianhuo, and started testing its effects on the newly recruited Dusk Guardians members throughout the years.

### Game Events

Yi first encounters Ji at the base of the Daybreak Tower in Lake Yaochi Ruins. Yi does not recognize Ji, as he taken a younger form, though Ji calls Yi a friend. The two speak, with Ji behaving in a rather cryptic way and explaining the history of the tower they stand in. Ji also gives Yi one of his prized possessions, the Ancient Sheet Music, which can be given to Ruyi to be transcribed into the Ancient Penglai Ballad.

Ji is next encountered in the left viewing area of the Boundless Repository. In this conversation, he explains that he knows why Yi is there and what he will do to Yanlao; however, he has no intentions of stopping Yi, and is simply there to view the repository and its treasure while it's still operational. Yi questions if Ji intends to convince him to not destroy the antiques of the Boundless Repository, but Ji immediately refutes this, expressing his apathy toward this endeavor.

Ji explains to Yi that while most believe that the beauty of antiques can somehow be recreated by preserving them, Ji disagrees with this notion. He recounts the example of the Parting Missive, the final work of Jietong, the final emperor of the Jie Kingdom. He reminiscences how Jietong was both an ambitious sovereign and an outstanding calligrapher, and describes how Jietong wrote the Parting Massive just as Lear and his Fangshi Guild began to storm Jietong's palace. Shortly after the completion of the missive, Jietong took his own life. According to Ji, "the overflowing vitality behind [Jietong's] words [could] only be felt in the very moment he put those words to paper", and future generations would neither be able to emulate nor reproduce the beauty of Jietong's final words.

Unaware of Ji's true identity, Yi disputes the validity of Ji's example, stating that Ji's interpretation of the event is his romantic fantasy of ancient times, and that nobody can prove whether or not the Parting Missive was even written by Jietong. Amused, Ji agrees, and expresses his opinion that the Parting Missive need not be immortalized within history. He compares the beauty of ancient relics to fireworks in the night sky: much like fireworks, the true beauty held by ancient relics can only be experienced for a fleeting moment.

Soon Ji questions why one should cling to eternal beauty compared to magnificent but fleeting moments, leading Yi to reason that people find it difficult to let go of certain things. Ji then queries why letting go is difficult, when eventually the end comes for everyone. His response prompts Yi to call him a pessimist. However, Ji rejects the label of pessimist, stating that he doesn't speculate, and instead speaks of what he already knows.

Yi questions if what Ji knows is related to his supernatural powers of divination, and demands that if Ji is so certain about the accuracy of his divination, then he should tell Yi about what's to come next. Ji gladly obliges, foretelling that the containers and treasures contained in the Boundless Repository will eventually be buried, and that they will form the grave that houses Yanlao. Yi questions the validity of the prophecy and disparages its value to him, then threatens to never speak to Ji again if he catches Ji tipping Yanlao off. Ji explains that he has no intentions of doing so, as Yanlao's fate is set, and encourages Yi to verify the fortune for himself, bidding Yi farewell and vanishing. As promised, Ji's divination becomes true; Yanlao perishes to his late-stage Tianhuo infection following his battle with Yi.

Some time before Jiequan encounters Yi for the first time, Ji visits Jiequan to inform him of the results of his divination: that Yi will be responsible for Jiequan meeting his end. Later, after Jiequan imprisons Yi, Ji visits Jiequan again in Jiequan's chamber. Jiequan asserts that Ji's predictions didn't materialize, stating that he took care of Yi, and that Ji's fatalism doesn't work on him. Ji responds that the fate he has witnessed for Jiequan has not changed.

Weary of discussing the matter, Jiequan diverges from the topic and tells Ji about his intent to eliminate anyone he believes is an obstacle. Ji questions if his actions are intended to restore the Jie dynasty, but Jiequan declares that his actions are to protect the Council instead, as he considers threats to the Council to be equivalent to threats to the Jie family. Reaching the time for a scheduled transmutation session, Jiequan informs Ji about his upcoming session and laments the atrophying of his muscles. Ji expresses his disbelief that Jiequan managed to become stronger using transmutation but Jiequan rebukes this notion, proclaiming that people who believe that transmutation goes against the laws of nature are alarmists and cowards. Jiequan bids farewell to Ji, and Ji vanishes from the chamber.

As foretold, Jiequan perishes during his battle with Yi, after undergoing too much transmutation and liquefying his body.

When Yi enters the Grotto of Scriptures, Ji in his younger form greets Yi at the [entrance of the area](Grotto_of_Scriptures_(Entry) "wikilink"). Here, Ji explains some of the history of the Grotto of Scriptures, and that while he is capable of divination, he is unable to reveal the intentions behind actions. Ji also reveals that he knows Yi better than he previously let on, confusing Yi. Ji claims that he knows who Yi is because Yi is the "new savior of the solarians", but Yi rejects this explanation, deducing that Ji isn't a normal solarian. Yi asks if Ji is a descendant of the Ancient Kunlun Tribe, a special tribe of solarians that had evacuated from New Kunlun before it had taken off. Many members of the tribe were rumored to possess special abilities, leading Yi to believe that Ji descending from this tribe would explain his precognition.

Ji expresses surprise at Yi's inquiry, and asks if Yi is finally ready to admit that Ji's precognition isn't just superstition. Yi reluctantly concedes, as although he is unsure about the exact details behind Ji's abilities, the results of Ji's precognition appear to be real. In response, Ji offers to read the Fangshi's fortune, which Yi begrudgingly accepts as it could yield useful information. To read Yi's fortune, Ji has him close his eyes and clear his thoughts. With Yi's eyes closed, Ji reads his fortune, and is left awestruck by the fortune he has determined.

Cryptically, Ji mumbles to himself about Yi and remarks that he has "only observed a divergence among a handful of souls". Yi tells Ji that he has a tendency of saying things that no one would understand, and asks Ji what he was mumbling about. Ji apologizes to Yi, informing him that the secret he observed cannot be divulged. Insulted by the secrecy, Yi demands to receive an explanation. Promising that he will, Ji invites Yi to visit him in his royal hall within the depths of the grotto. The invite to a royal hall shocks Yi, leading him to realize Ji's identity as the Sol of the Grotto of Scriptures.

Upon reaching Ji's royal hall, Yi finds the oracle writing on a large stone slab, one of many in the area. Ji explains that this writing began as a casual hobby, but eventually evolved into a gargantuan record of the entire span of solarian history. Yi demands to know why Ji has been following him around New Kunlun and interacting with him. To this, Ji simply responds that he wished to know more about Yi, and had no greater motivations. Ji remarks that "great figures like [Yi] will always shine with a certain splendor", and that the splendor is one "not dimmed by mortality." Following this, Ji rises into the air and transforms, returning to his adult form before Yi's eyes.

Ji explains that perhaps due to a divine prank, age means nothing to him, revealing to Yi that he is immortal. In disbelief, Yi says that it's impossible, and questions how long Ji has lived. Letting out a soft laugh, Ji explains that he still doesn't understand how he's immortal, even after several lifetimes. He goes on to question why, out of anyone, he was made immortal. Although Ji possessed immortality, without any great ambitions or excessive desires of his own, Ji only ever stood on the sidelines of history as a bystander watching all the people he cared about leave him behind.

Yi comments that Ji's agelessness and immortality are abilities many could only dream about, and Ji agrees; however, Ji iterates that for himself, agelessness and immortalty feel more like a curse. He then laments that he lacks the courage to end his curse, ostensibly by taking his life.

The incredible nature of Ji's abilities leads Yi to question if Eigong was aware of Ji's immortality, and question why Ji decided to join the Tiandao Council. Yi especially questions the latter point, as the Tianhuo Virus doesn't appear to have an effect on Ji at all, meaning that the oracle has no self-preserving incentive to help discover a cure. Ji confesses that he joined the Council to avoid eternal loneliness on Penglai, and that he made a promise to a friend to protect the Grotto of Scriptures.

Bearing no ill intent toward Ji, Yi requests that Ji just hand over his Sol Seal, declaring to Ji that he will guarantee that the grotto will not be harmed in any way. Ji asks if Yi is going easy on him, but Yi explains otherwise: Yi emphasizes that he only needs Ji's Sol Seal, and that he doesn't want to fight Ji because Ji was barely a part of the Council. Furthermore, Yi wishes to avoid exterminating the Ancient Kunlun Tribe, as Ji is likely the last remaining member of the tribe.

Despite Yi's offer of a peaceful solution, Ji declares that he has "never disobeyed destiny", reminding Yi about the divination he performed earlier. Ji claims that in his divination, he foretold that Yi would die by his hand. Announcing his title as the Deputy Captain of the Fangshi Guild Elite Guard, Sojourner Ji, Ji commences a fight with Yi.

After Yi defeats Ji, Yi remarks that the oracle's hexagrams had failed him, as Ji was the one who would die rather than Yi. Ji remarks that "at least one of [them] got to enjoy the thrill of the unknown", revealing he had lied to Yi about the result of the divination. Instead, the divination had foretold Ji's death at the hands of Yi. When questioned by Yi as to why he lied, Ji responds that he lied in order to ensure that Yi performed his utmost, as knowing the true outcome could have affected Yi's performance. In his last moments, Ji begins to rapidly age as he reminiscences about the events he's seen over his lifetime. Soon Ji passes away, leaving behind a corpse uniquely unblemished by the pervasion of Tianhuo.

### Chamber

Ji's Vital Sanctum chamber inside the Ancient Stone Pillar, reflects his life as a bystander that has viewed all of solarian history. His chamber contains many items of the past: old scrolls, calligraphy, jade discs, and portraits - which includes an ancient portait of himself. Additionally, Ji also possesses an antique model of Shanhai assistive robot - the Shanhai 1000 - which is likely to be one of the first usable Shanhai models. Although to Ji these items are merely personal notes or casual keepsakes for himself, they are also immensely valuable artifacts that each embody a piece of solarian history.

## Boss

**Ji** is the Sol boss of Grotto of Scriptures. He holds the Seal of Insight.

### Combat Appearance

#### Young Form

- Shorter than Yi.
- Maroon priest’s hat with an inset gold trim that cuts into the center. Has a green metal band with a snake pattern with a large blue eye in the center. The hat has fabric that droops down to shoulders and end with gold chevron edges.
- Outfit is a cloak that follows the same colors as cap with gold hexagonal trims covering the bottom.
- Almost always has eyes closed, only opening them slightly sometimes showing blank white pupils with clear edges.

#### Adult Form

- Levitates in the air and extends limbs to regaining similar proportions to an adult solarian.
- Reveals clawed green fingernails and a slightly scruffy fur.
- Hat and cloak merge into one and spread outward.
- Reveals inner clothing, a gold and tan triangular cross shaped scapular that droops between legs, shoes are the same maroon as hat.
- Psychically holds an armillary sphere that contains space particles within it.
- Wields a khakkhara for crimson attacks.

- Divination Orbs of the same type can repeat in a single divination.

## Trivia

### Gameplay

- In second phase, after around 50 seconds, Ji will repeat his starting black hole attack.
- During phase 2, if you're on one side of Ji as he shoots a dagger before Crimson Slam and then quickly get behind him, the dagger will instead evade away as the Crimson Slam starts to give player time to charge Unbounded Counter.
- While players are allowed to complete the Empyrean District and Grotto of Scriptures in whichever order they choose, choosing to complete the Grotto of Scriptures and Ji's fight before visiting the Empyrean District creates a peculiar plotline.
  - If the Empyrean District and the Fengs are completed first, Yi can obtain the Tianhuo Serum present in Nuwa's chamber and present it for to Ruyi for analysis. Ruyi informs Yi that this serum acts as a catalyst for a new mutation of the Tianhuo virus. When Yi asks if there is a way for them to deal with the Tianhuo Mutants, Ruyi informs him that his analyses have determined that they can defeat the mutants by permanently destroying the genetic sequence in them that is responsible for their regeneration. However, Ruyi is unable to trace this genetic sequence, as mutants are amalgmations of multiple genetic sources; the only way to trace it would be to find the original cells responsible. The only hint they have as to the identity of the person with this original genetic sequence is that they are a solarian that ages very slowly. This knowledge prompts Yi to try and identify who the original genetic sequence belongs to, later giving him a reason to collect Ji's Hair, as Ji fits the profile of the individual whose genetic sequence was used in the mutants.
  - If the Grotto of Scriptures and Ji is completed first, Yi is still allowed to obtain Ji's hair despite not possessing this aforementioned knowledge. As such, by completing the game in this order, Yi collects Ji's hair for seemingly no reason at first.

### Lore

- Many important people during the Zhou Dynasty have the surname [**Ji (姬)**](wp:Ji_(Zhou_dynasty_ancestral_surname) "wikilink"), however he is most likely a reference to Ji Chang (姬昌), a ruler of the Zhou state in ancient China. Renowned for his wisdom, benevolence, and effective governance, Ji Chang laid the groundwork for the overthrowing of the Shang Dynasty. He is often credited with significant contributions to Chinese culture and political thought, particularly for his role in the development of the I Ching (Book of Changes), a foundational text for divination and philosophy in China. The I Ching, which includes a system of hexagrams used for divination, reflects his insights into the natural and moral order of the universe.
- Ji's appearance resembles a Scottish Fold cat.
- The legible portion of the solarian text that Ji is writing on the tablet in the manga cutscene before the fight translates to "buried here", likely referencing him to be buried in the Grotto with his story now attaining wholeness with the Great Tao.
- One of the gifts Yi can give to Shuanshuan is the Kunlun Immortal Portrait, drawn by the fled literati. It alludes that the Kunlun Immortal is Ji, portrayed with the same headdress Ji wore in his youth (as shown in his soulscape memory), his cloak with diamond patterns and the item description calling them an ageless immortal. The treasure the hidden poem on the back of the painting hints to is also stashed at Ji's whereabouts – Grotto of Scriptures.
- According to the Nine Sols Artbook, to match his otherworldly aura, Ji was designed with many Buddhist motifs. The pattern found on his attire is derived from the "Five-Buddha Crown", a kind of crown worn by Tibetan Buddhist monks during rituals or public ceremonial performances. Another example of Buddhist motifs are Ji's tools, which are inspired by Buddhist ritual implements, such as his spear bearing remarkable similarities to a Khakkhara.
- Ji is not immortal in the sense of being invincible / undying, but in the matter of having eternal life / biological immortality. Ji says before the battle: *"Due perhaps to some divine prank, age means nothing to me. [...] How could this be possible, right? I don’t understand it either, even after several lifetimes."*
  - This supernatural ability also allows him to somehow morph his body to his childhood or adulthood state, and likely other ages. When dying, his body also morphs into very old age.
