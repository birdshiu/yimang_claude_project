**Yi** (traditional Chinese: 羿; pinyin: *Yì*) is the protagonist and player-controlled character of Nine Sols. He was one of the ten Sols of the Tiandao Council, holding the Sol Seal of Innovation.

## Appearance

### Adult

Yi is a short solarian in his early adulthood. Characters often make comments regarding his diminutive height and youthful appearance:

- Shuanshuan asks him if he was transmutated because other solarian looks taller than him, to which he replies that it has nothing to do with gene modification;
- Shanhai 9000 in the Empyrean District (Living Area) remarks that Yi does not look like a legal adult;
- He is one of the shortest characters in the game, being approximately as tall as Shuanshuan, Yanlao, Ji in his young form and slightly taller than a few children of the Peach Blossom Village.

His body is fully covered by pale yellow or cream smooth fur, the length of which seems to be medium-hair.

Yi has large, narrow eyes with dark brown iris, black pupils and a small black nose.

His mouth is omitted or covered by fur as a stylistic choice, but the narrative implies that Yi has it:

- No character ever points out Yi missing a mouth, unlike his age or height;
- He's able to drink;
- Able to eat;
- Inhales the Medicine Pipe and exhales the smoke afterwards;
- Coughs up blood when hurt.

Yi has sharp straight ears that, when scanned with x-ray, contain, presumably, ear bones or some kind of implant (perhaps for receiving transmissions, since he brings up a hand near his ear); these bones or implants do not appear on any of the solarian skeletal corpses.

There are two conical fur appendages protruding in opposite directions on each side of his head, they sway slightly during movement. Unlike the ears, they do not have any bones.

Yi almost constantly wears a furrowed expression.

Yi wears a loose vibrant yellow Fangshi robe with grayish brown hems. His Innovation Sol Seal insignia is woven on the shoulders and a Fusang symbol is woven on the back, which are also grayish brown color. The robe's sleeves are long enough to hide his arms entirely and it extends down to his knees. Its loose cuts grant freedom of movement and basic protection. Yi's choice of color for his own robe accentuates his free-spirited nature.

Underneath, Yi is dressed in a skin-tight black body suit. It does cover his hands, they are human-like and have claws. He wears white socks and black taoist shoes with white outsole. His whole body suit, socks and shoes can decontruct and reconstruct, likely with Azure Sand nano-technology, similarly to Jiequan's armor, with the exception of his Fangshi robe.

At the center of his chest, directly over and notched into his heart (the x-ray scan shows a hole in Yi's rib cage where it is located) is a large jade ring called Rhizomatic Stabilizer, that serves as a medical device for his unique heart, that has merged with Primordial Roots in an incident, granting him extraordinary abilities, such as being able to store Qi, increased regeneration and potentially slower aging and ability to be reborn from the Roots after death.

The design of the hats Eigong and Yi wear in the beginning act of the game is inspired by the Chinese Weimao from the Tang dynasty. **Weimao** (Chinese: 帷帽; lit. 'veiled hat or curtained hat') is a type of wide-brimmed hat with a shoulder-length hanging veil that evolved from the full-body veil Mili hat by the end of the Sui dynasty.

In the flashbacks, at his homeland, Xia, he was dressed in an opened loose green-blue plain taoist robe with light green hems.

### Young

As a child, Yi was overall a little smaller, and lacks the furrowed brows he has as an adult.

He wore a closed plain taoist robe with light green hems and dark green loose pants. Simple and practical, it matches with his sister's.

He distinctly lacks the jade ring on his chest.

## Story

### Early Life

Yi was born to a Taoist family in the Xia Kingdom alongside his little sister Heng in a village near an ocean. As a child, Yi was very focused on and proficient in science and technology. He was not very social, often choosing to read or study instead of interacting with his peers, But he wasn't lonely because he remained close to his young sister Heng whom adored and followed him. Although Yi ignored Heng's ability to communicate to nature (including Fusang), thinking that she was delusional and into a hobby of making up stories because she had no friends, he hung out with her and gave her the science stories he knew, hoping Heng would realize the merits of science.

However, his parents revered Taoism, did not respect Yi's will because they had a strong negative prejudice against science. Yi was growing to resent his parents' traditional views on the dangers of science, and becoming estranged from his parents, but He wanted to be recognized by his parents by proving the usefulness of science with his ability.

Yi's talent in science led him to take on the project of constructing the Azure Flyer (renamed to Mystic Nymph by Heng) for a local science fair in order to impress the scientists visiting it. At the fair, Eigong, the scientist Yi admired, was going to visit for a lecture, so with the goal of getting Eigong's attention, Yi set out to develop Azure Flyer for a month with Heng as his assistant.

Finally, at dawn on the day of the fair, the Mystic Nymph was nearly complete, only requiring one last charge of rhizomatic energy. Yi was warned by Heng that the Primordial Roots were "suffocating," and that something was terribly wrong, but Yi, distrustful of Heng's more personified understanding of the Roots, did not heed her warning and initiated the charging. However, as she predicted, the procedure went very wrong, with Yi barely managing to tackle Heng out of the way before the charging machine exploded. In the aftermath of the blast, Heng suffered no major injuries, but the left half of Yi's body was entirely destroyed, leaving his heart exposed. For an unknown reason, perhaps due to Heng crying over her brother's corpse, the Roots began to envelop the two, forming some sort of green barrier around them.

After that, Yi was transported to Eigong, who was fascinated by his miraculous survival due to the Roots, and received professional treatment through her. A rhizomatic stabilizer, appearing from the outside as a green ring, was implanted into Yi's chest to keep his heart stable as he somehow recovered from his injuries. Despite the dramatic failure of the apparatus, the charging and creation of the Mystic Nymph was a success, and this impressed Eigong enough to convince her to take Yi in and mentor him.

So, Yi entered the Tiandao Academy as Eigong's student and spent time under her tutelage, honing his talents and learning Fangshi martial arts from her. He also met Kuafu, quickly becoming best friends with him. Five years later, Yi was chosen as a Sol in the Tiandao Council, bearing the Sol Seal of Innovation.

### Life as a Sol

As a Sol, Yi developed a rapport with a number of the Council members, being most friendly with Lady Ethereal and especially so with Kuafu, who considered Yi to be like a brother to him. Of course, there were those who were jealous of him (Goumang), or whom he didn't want to get close because of cruel personality and bad national history relations (Jiequan), and immature aristocratic twins who easily became the Sols because they were sponsors of council (Fengs), but although either tolerated or somewhat disliked, Yi tried to respect them as well as Sols.

Yi's major contribution as a Sol was the Eternal Cauldron Project, a radical plan to buy time in the search for the cure for Tianhuo. The Eternal Cauldron Project would see the island of Kunlun, along with the Primordial Root network embedded in it, warped 500 light-years away from Penglai to the solar system containing the Pale Blue Planet, discovered to host intelligent life by the Haotian Sphere. Apemen from the Pale Blue Planet were to be captured and brought to New Kunlun to be farmed and their brains harvested. The apeman brains were to be incorporated into a massive biological computer that would run the soulscapes of all 99,999 solarians on the island, keeping them safe in stasis as work on the cure was underway. Having very few other options, the Council accepted the Eternal Cauldron Project as the plan of action and began preparations for the launch of New Kunlun.

Yi visited his hometown and begged his family to join the project. However, his parents refused to be associated with it and told Yi that he should follow Taoism's course. They also opposed his request to allow Heng to be with him, citing previous accidents, and refused him, saying "you're not to harm her anymore". Eventually, while he was working on the project, Yi's parents died after contracting the Tianhuo virus, He couldn't keep his parents from dying and had to pay tribute to their graves late after the funeral.

Yi wanted to protect Heng, now his only living family member, so he approached Heng about having her come onto the New Kunlun that she would not die alone on Penglai. However, Heng didn't answer that his offer. she believed Tianhuo was a transition to return to the Great Tao, and that this was the path for all things to return to nature, so just wanted Yi to accept her death and get on with his life. Yi was furious at this but decided to wait to try to convince her once more, hoping that she might change her mind.

But when that time came, Heng refused Yi's offer. Yi was puzzled by how Heng could possibly, in his eyes, throw her life away like that. However, When Heng accused him that all of his accomplishments were not for his family and other Solarians, but for the satisfaction of his desire for recognition and self-satisfaction, Yi was frustrated that he was not understood by his sister that he wanted to protect. Nevertheless, he completely resolved to give Heng one more chance, threatening to disown her if she refused a third time.

However, before Yi got the chance to ask Heng a third time, he learned of the origin of Tianhuo, and how Eigong had created it. He felt betrayed that he was the only Sol without this knowledge, and entered a fierce argument with Eigong that culminated in a fight. Yi was defeated, falling off of the cliff above the Forbidden Cave to his death. However, after his broken body reached the bottom of the cave, the Roots grabbed him, dragging him into the wall of the cave, where they slowly healed him.

~500 years later, Yi is discovered by Shuanshuan, who had very recently lost both of his parents to the harvest ceremony. Shuanshuan cares for Yi, helping him relearn how to walk, until he makes a recovery. Yi acts as Shuanshuan's surrogate older brother for 2 years in Peach Blossom Village, hiding his identity from the other villagers.

### Game Events

The game begins with Yi interrupting the harvest ceremony, for which Shuanshuan was chosen. Instead of riding the platform down into the Apeman Facility after all 3 apemen were harvested, which was the plan, Yi saves Shuanshuan from being harvested and then descends the platform, which triggers multiple alarms. Yi's goal is motivated by revenge: to slay the other nine Sols for wronging him, and to acquire the Council Code, composed of all ten Sol Seals. He progresses to the Four Seasons Pavilion, where he activates Ruyi and meets up with Shuanshuan, who Yi allows to reside in the Pavilion.

Yi then proceeds to face the first 3 Sols, recruiting Kuafu and defeating Yanlao and Goumang. Yi is then captured and imprisoned by Jiequan, who tortures Yi and sends an attack onto Peach Blossom Village. Yi escapes the Prison, but collapses after exiting Factory (Machine Room), being brought back to the Pavilion by Chiyou. When Yi awakes and recovers, he continues onto his path towards Jiequan, defeating him. His encounter with Jiequan and the things that Jiequan said to him during his imprisonment cause Yi to begin to reflect on the purpose of his mission, that perhaps his drive for revenge is misguided.

Yi enters Lady Ethereal's soulscape, and within learns that the soulscape system, an integral part of the Eternal Cauldron Project, never actually functioned properly, and that Lady Ethereal had lied to the Council about this because of the dire circumstances. He is hesitant to kill her, but Lady Ethereal's overwhelming feeling of guilt forces her to fight, where she is defeated by Yi. He then moves on to defeat The Fengs and Ji. Yi actively attempts to avoid combat with them, but they choose to fight, sealing their fates. The items that Yi obtains from defeating them allow Kuafu and Ruyi to create the Super Mutant Buster, a device that opens the way in the Tiandao Research Center to access Eigong.

Upon entering Eigong's Vital Sanctum, Yi finds Eigong there, waiting for him. With the power she has over those in her own soulscape, she seizes all 8 Sol Seals that Yi has collected, and strands him in the soulscape. For an unknown reason, he is eventually able to escape, and has to fight his way through the Tianhuo Research Institute to reach the New Kunlun Central Hall.

At the Root Pinnacle Yi reaches the culmination of his journey and achieves one of two **Endings** of the game.

## Abilities

Yi boasts a wide variety of abilities throughout the course of the game, inspired by Tai-Chi martial arts and spell-casting gestures used in Taoism.

## Trivia

### Gameplay

- The final boss fight that Red Candle originally wanted was for Yi to reveal his true colors. The fight was a pair of black and white Yi's fighting, and after the fight, they would continue to fight against the Tianhuo collective. However, the development cost was too expensive and the script encountered many problems. As a result, this concept was scrapped.

### Lore

- In the first version of the Nine Sols script, Yi and Tianhuo were all attributed to nature as determined by Tao. Yi was a darker, contemptible person.
- Lear calls Yi a fellow disciple — daoshi (Chinese: 道士; lit. 'scholar of the Tao'), translated as Taoist priest, Taoist monk, or Taoist professional. The courtesy title of a senior daoshi is daozhang (道长, meaning "Tao master"), and a highly accomplished and revered **daoshi** is often called a zhenren (真人, "perfected person").
- It is implied that after the defeat in the battle against Eigong at the start of the game, while sustained by the Primordial Roots in the mountain wall, Yi did not age physically at all, while Vital Sanctums technology only slows down the aging of other solarians. Ruyi is puzzled by how Yi was able to maintain his youth over "all these years" and the new-found information that Primordial Roots can grant everlasting youth. When presented with a question about solarian with very slow aging, Kuafu asks if it could be the imprisoned Kanghui or, perhaps, **Yi**, who "haven't aged ever since the incident", and says that he has the most enigmatic body in all of New Kunlun.
- Internal damage that is in the game is called **"內傷"** - internal injury. In addition to physical damage, it can also mean that the flow of Qi in the body becomes chaotic and difficult to control. It may cause difficulty breathing in mild cases, but may cause internal bleeding or even damage to internal organs in severe cases.

### Inspirations

- Yi's character is inspired by the myth of Hou Yi, in which the archer Hou Yi shot down nine suns with his bow so that only one would remain.
- The finger gestures that Yi and Lear use to summon a sword made of Qi is inspired by common spell-casting gesture (**劍指** - sword fingers) in Taoism, used to represent the "sword" that can kill evil creatures. Later, this gesture was also widely used in Shintoism in Japan. Sometimes Taoist priests would transfer the Qi in their bodies to their hands through the meridians, and then concentrate it on their fingertips and radiate it out.
